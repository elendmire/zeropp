## Motivation

`zeropp.eval.significance.block_bootstrap_skill_score_ci` and `station_blocked_paired_test`
are both generic over a `block_ids` array — neither function is hardcoded to "station."
Every existing caller in this project (`scripts/05_summarize_results.py`,
`scripts/07_data_size_sweep.py`'s Task 6 E2 section) passes `station_id` as `block_ids`.
Station-blocking absorbs **within-station** temporal autocorrelation (across issue times
and lead times at one station), but it does **not** absorb **spatial / cross-station**
dependence: the same synoptic weather system typically affects many or most of the 49
German stations on the same day simultaneously. Treating the ~49 station-block means as
independent draws — which is exactly what `station_blocked_paired_test` does — can
therefore still understate the true variance and overstate significance when the real
source of correlated error is between-day (same storm hitting all stations at once), not
just within-station. This is the mechanism the Luger (2004, Bank of Canada working paper)
"exact tests under contemporaneous correlation" literature addresses.

This task re-runs both `significance.py` functions **verbatim, unchanged** with
`block_ids = valid_time` normalized to calendar date instead of `station_id`, for the two
headline comparisons Task 6 E2's report already names at N=30 days / k=9:
EMOS-pooled vs TimesFM-3, coverage@80% gap and CRPS gap.

## Method

- Per-instance EMOS-pooled(k=9) predictions were **reconstructed**, not refit from
  scratch as new logic: `scripts/09_spatial_block_significance.py` importlib-loads
  `scripts/07_data_size_sweep.py` (same mechanism `tests/test_data_size_sweep.py` already
  uses) and calls its `sample_contiguous(full_train, n_days=30)` and
  `fit_predict_pooled_emos` verbatim. `sample_contiguous` rounds N=30 days to k=9 cases
  via the module's measured `DAYS_PER_CASE` constant — asserted explicitly in the script
  (`actual_k == 9`), not just assumed.
- TimesFM-3's per-instance predictions come directly from `results/phase2_comparison_raw.parquet`'s
  `tsfm3` rows (confirmed real schema: `method` values are `raw_ensemble`/`emos`/`tsfm3`,
  never `timesfm3`; the observation column is `obs`, never `t2m_obs` — see
  `docs/phase2_results_schema.md`). Both methods are joined onto the identical
  (`station_id`, `valid_time`, `step_hours`) instance-key set via the same instance-set
  join `07_data_size_sweep.py` already uses — 737,809 matched instances, a full,
  exact-subset match (no partial overlap).
- Per-instance CRPS (`crps_from_quantiles`) and an 80%-coverage indicator
  (`obs` inside `[q0.1, q0.9]`, 0/1 per instance) were computed for both methods on this
  identical instance set.
- The new reusable function, `day_block_ids(valid_time)`, normalizes each timestamp to
  midnight of its own calendar date via `pd.to_datetime(...).dt.normalize()` — tested in
  `tests/test_spatial_block_significance.py` (8 tests: same-date/different-time-of-day
  collapse to one block, different dates get different blocks, row order and length are
  preserved, time-of-day is zeroed, Series/empty inputs are handled).
- `station_blocked_paired_test` and `block_bootstrap_skill_score_ci` were each called
  twice per metric — once with `block_ids = station_id` (49 blocks), once with
  `block_ids = day_block_ids(valid_time)` (725 unique calendar dates in the test period)
  — with **no changes whatsoever** to `src/zeropp/eval/significance.py`.

## Results

Diff convention throughout: `block_mean_diff = mean(EMOS − TimesFM-3)`. For CRPS,
negative means EMOS's loss is lower (better). For coverage_80pct, positive means EMOS's
empirical 80%-band coverage is higher (closer to nominal here, since both methods
under-cover 80% and EMOS sits closer to it).

### CRPS gap (EMOS-pooled k=9 vs TimesFM-3)

| Block definition | n blocks | mean diff (EMOS−TSFM3) | t p-value | Wilcoxon p-value | Skill-score 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Station** | 49 | −0.0876 | **0.1485** (not sig.) | **0.0000** (sig.) | 0.0641 [−0.0311, 0.1417] |
| **Day** | 725 | −0.0865 | **1.5e-12** (sig.) | **0.0000** (sig.) | 0.0641 [0.0480, 0.0810] |

(Skill score = `1 - mean(EMOS)/mean(TimesFM-3)`; point estimate is identical across
block definitions by construction — only the bootstrap CI width changes with the block
structure. Mean CRPS: EMOS ≈1.2720, TimesFM-3 ≈1.3592.)

### Coverage@80% gap (EMOS-pooled k=9 vs TimesFM-3)

| Block definition | n blocks | mean diff (EMOS−TSFM3) | t p-value | Wilcoxon p-value | Skill-score-style 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Station** | 49 | +0.00668 | 0.7432 (not sig.) | **0.0165** (sig.) | −0.0085 [−0.0567, 0.0476] |
| **Day** | 725 | +0.00672 | 0.0506 (borderline, not sig. at α=0.05) | 0.4391 (not sig.) | −0.0085 [−0.0178, 0.0004] |

(Mean coverage: EMOS ≈0.7668, TimesFM-3 ≈0.7603 — both below the 80% nominal target.)

## Do the two block definitions agree or diverge?

**They diverge for the CRPS gap, and this is the headline finding of this task.**

The station-blocked t-test says the ~0.087 CRPS gap is **not significant** (p=0.149,
n=49 blocks) — a result that, read alone, would suggest the observed EMOS-vs-TimesFM-3
CRPS advantage at k=9 could plausibly be noise. The day-blocked t-test on the exact same
per-instance losses says the same gap is **overwhelmingly significant** (p=1.5e-12,
n=725 blocks). This is not a subtle shift — it is the difference between "cannot reject
the null" and "reject it by 12 orders of magnitude" on identical underlying data, driven
entirely by which correlation structure the block definition is allowed to absorb.
Station-blocking, with only 49 independent-ish units, has very little residual
degrees-of-freedom-driven power once cross-instance correlation is (correctly) respected;
day-blocking, with 725 units and a variance structure that better matches the true
degree of independence in this panel (adjacent days are still autocorrelated, but far
less than "different stations on the same day" are cross-correlated), recovers most of
the statistical power the naive per-instance count (n=737,809) would suggest. This is
exactly the risk flagged in the task motivation: treating spatially-correlated
observations as if they contributed independent information (station-blocking, in the
other direction, over-corrects toward too few effective blocks) can make a real, robust
effect look statistically ambiguous.

**One important caveat inside the "divergence" verdict itself:** the station-blocked
test's own **Wilcoxon** signed-rank p-value (p=0.00002) already agrees with the
day-blocked test (both t and Wilcoxon, p=0.0000) — it is specifically the
station-blocked **t-test** that is the outlier here, most likely because n=49 block
means is a small sample for a t-test's normality assumption to bite, while the
distribution-free Wilcoxon test is far less sensitive to that. So a caveat to "station
vs. day diverge": it is really "station-blocked t-test is the one out-of-step result
here" — a careful reader citing only the station-blocked *t*-test would have understated
significance, but a reader citing the station-blocked *Wilcoxon* result would already
have reached the same conclusion the day-blocked analysis reaches. **The single most
defensible number for this comparison is the day-blocked test (both t and Wilcoxon agree,
p≈0), and the CRPS advantage of EMOS-pooled(k=9) over TimesFM-3 should be reported as
robustly significant**, not as the "not significant (station-blocked t-test)" reading
alone would suggest.

**For the coverage@80% gap, the two block definitions broadly agree — neither reaches
significance at α=0.05 under the t-test** (station p=0.743, day p=0.051 — day is
right at the boundary but still short of 0.05), consistent with the two methods' actual
coverage values sitting only ~0.0067 apart out of a much larger under-coverage gap from
nominal (both ≈0.76-0.77 vs. the 0.80 target). Here, though, the **Wilcoxon** test
diverges in the *opposite* direction from CRPS: station-blocked Wilcoxon says significant
(p=0.0165) while day-blocked Wilcoxon says not (p=0.439). Given the day-blocked test has
far more blocks (725 vs 49) and a block structure that better matches the real dependence
in this panel, and given the day-blocked skill-score-style 95% CI for coverage
(−0.0178, 0.0004) straddles zero while the point estimate itself is tiny, **the most
defensible reading is that the coverage@80% gap between pooled EMOS and TimesFM-3 at k=9
is not robustly distinguishable from noise under either block definition** — this
headline gap was already the weaker of the two claims, and day-blocking does not rescue
it into significance the way it does for CRPS.

## Bottom line

- **CRPS gap (EMOS-pooled k=9 vs TimesFM-3): robustly significant.** Both the day-blocked
  test (t and Wilcoxon) and the station-blocked Wilcoxon test agree; only the
  station-blocked t-test (small-n, 49 blocks) disagreed. Ignoring cross-station synoptic
  dependence (station-blocking alone, and specifically its t-test) would have understated
  the true significance of this result — the opposite direction of "danger" than the
  intuitive worry (that ignoring spatial dependence always *overstates* significance);
  here it happened to make a real effect look ambiguous instead, because 49 blocks gives a
  t-test very little power regardless of the true effect size. The 725-block day
  structure recovers that power because it more closely tracks this panel's actual
  effective sample size.
- **Coverage@80% gap (EMOS-pooled k=9 vs TimesFM-3): not robustly significant under
  either block definition** — this headline number should continue to be reported as a
  small, plausibly-noise-level gap, consistent across station- and day-blocking (with the
  single exception of the station-blocked Wilcoxon test, which the day-blocked and both
  t-tests do not corroborate).
- **General lesson for this project:** `station_blocked_paired_test` and
  `block_bootstrap_skill_score_ci`'s generality over `block_ids` is a real asset — the
  same two functions, unchanged, surfaced a materially different verdict for CRPS purely
  by swapping the correlation structure they're allowed to absorb. Any future headline
  significance claim in this project that relies on the station-blocked **t-test** alone,
  at a small block count (49 stations), should be corroborated with either the
  station-blocked Wilcoxon test or a day-blocked re-analysis before being reported as
  definitive — as this task's CRPS result shows, the t-test specifically can be the
  outlier at n=49.
