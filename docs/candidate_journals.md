## On arXiv-first culture

Confirmed: this exact subfield (ML postprocessing of ensemble weather forecasts) has an active, standard arXiv-first culture — e.g. arXiv:2309.04452 ("Postprocessing of Ensemble Weather Forecasts Using Permutation-invariant Neural Networks") was later published in AIES, and several other closely related preprints exist (2303.17610, 2306.15956, 2005.08748). Posting to arXiv before or alongside journal submission is normal and expected in this community, not unusual for a first-time or solo author — arXiv has no author-affiliation gate at all.

**Would arXiv alone be a reasonable standalone outcome?** For a strong solo undergraduate project, arXiv-only is a completely legitimate, zero-cost, zero-barrier way to establish priority and get real visibility and citability immediately — several of the search results show serious, well-cited work living as arXiv preprints. But a peer-reviewed journal publication (even months later) carries materially more weight for things like grad school applications, since it means the work survived independent review. **The two are not mutually exclusive and not in tension**: post to arXiv immediately once the analysis is solid, then submit the same (or a revised) manuscript to a journal — this is exactly the community's own norm, not a workaround.

## Recommendation

1. **Post to arXiv first, regardless of any journal decision** — free, fast, establishes priority, and is the field's own norm.
2. **Primary journal target: QJRMS**, specifically because standard (non-open-access) publication has **no fee at all** — the only real venue here with zero cost for a student with no funding, while still being the most topically authoritative venue (the postprocessing literature's own home journal).
3. **Backup target: AIES**, because it has an explicit, usable fee-waiver mechanism for genuine financial need, is fully open access (more visible than QJRMS's subscription model), and is a strong topical fit.
4. Treat **Machine Learning: Earth** as a third option only if QJRMS/AIES don't work out, given its cost is real even after the likely 30%-tier discount and it's an unproven new journal.
5. Do NOT target npj Climate and Atmospheric Science as a first attempt — cost and novelty bar are both mismatched for a first solo submission; revisit only after an initial publication track record exists.
6. The NeurIPS 2026 CCAI workshop deadline already passed (2026-08-29) — not available for this specific paper unless the workshop reopens a late track; watch for the next cycle (NeurIPS/ICLR 2027 climate workshops) as a free, fast, low-barrier option for a different or follow-up piece of work.

## Caveats / not independently verified

- QJRMS timeline, GMD's exact Turkey-eligibility for R4L/EED discount tiers, and AIES/Weather & Forecasting's exact current per-page AMS rate were not confirmed with a primary source in this pass — verify directly on the publisher sites before final submission.
- "Solo-author norm" assessments are inferred from general knowledge of each community/waiver policy language, not from a systematic scan of each journal's recent table of contents for solo-author papers — worth a quick manual spot-check (e.g., searching each journal's recent issues for single-author papers) before committing.
